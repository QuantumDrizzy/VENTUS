# VENTUS-1 — Design point

**Revision:** r4 (2026-08-27) · supersedes r3
**Change vs r3: RE-BASELINE from M 3.00 / 24 km to M 3.50 / 26 km.** A deliberate
change of aircraft, not a tweak — see §0 for what moved and why.
§1 and §2 are generated by M1 (`cargo run -p ventus-atmos --example table`).

**Condition:** M = 3.50, h = 26 000 m geopotential, U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976.
**Baseline gas model:** calorically perfect air, γ = 1.4, R = R*/M₀ = 287.05307
J·kg⁻¹·K⁻¹ (derived from the US76 pair, not the commonly quoted 287.0528).
Validity bounds and the correction are in §3 and in ADR-000 D10.

## 0. Why M 3.5, and what the re-baseline changed

M 3.75–3.78 was considered and rejected. The deciding argument was not thermal;
it was **the founding rule of this project: no yardstick, no module.**

| Vehicle | Mach | Altitude | Usable as an anchor at… |
|---|---|---|---|
| XB-70 Valkyrie | 3.0 | ~21 km | M 3.0 |
| SR-71 | 3.2 | ~24 km | M 3.2, extrapolates to 3.5 |
| D-21B drone (ramjet, titanium) | ~3.35 | ~27 km **[TO CITE]** | **the closest anchor to M 3.5** |
| *nothing public between here and scramjets* | | | |
| X-43 / X-51 | 7–10 | ~30 km | a different regime entirely |

At M 3.76 the SR-71 thrust-split anchor (54 % inlet / 17 % engine) becomes an
extrapolation rather than a check, and M3+M4 would have no external yardstick at
all. M 3.5 sits 0.15 Mach from the D-21 and 0.3 from the SR-71 — close enough
that both remain checks.

### What the re-baseline changed

| Quantity | M 3.0 / 24 km (r3) | **M 3.5 / 26 km (r4)** | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| q∞ | 18.462 kPa | **18.463 kPa** | **unchanged** — structural loading is identical |
| T₀ (thermally perfect) | 612.0 K | **752.8 K** | +141 K |
| T_aw (turbulent) | 296 °C | **421 °C** | the material answer flips — §3.3 |
| γ correction on T₀ | −5.7 K (−0.9 %) | **−15.3 K (−2.0 %)** | ADR-000 D10 matters more |
| Inlet recovery π_d | 0.809 | **0.742** | worse |
| Gain over a normal shock | ×2.46 | **×3.48** | **the inlet matters more, not less** |
| T₄max/T₀ | 2.78 | **2.26** | the turbomachine is further out of its element |
| Target L/D | 5.5–6.5 | **5.0–6.0** | §4 |

The dynamic-pressure coincidence is worth stating plainly: **26 km was chosen to
hold q, and it holds it to 0.004 %.** This re-baseline therefore does not touch
the structural loads case at all. It hardens exactly two things — the thermal
problem and the propulsive one — which is the point of doing it.

## 3. Thermal state — the result that selects the material

### 3.1 Stagnation temperature

At T₀ ≈ 750 K the vibrational modes of O₂ and N₂ are populating and cp is no
longer constant. The naive correction — re-evaluating γ at T₀ and reusing the
isentropic formula — is a **methodological error**: `T₀/T = 1 + (γ−1)/2·M²` is
algebraically identical to ΔT = V²/(2·cp) with **constant** cp, so feeding it the
hot γ charges the hot cp over the cold leg.

The correct condition is energy conservation with real enthalpy:

```
h₀ = h∞ + V²/2
h(222.65 K) = 222.63 kJ/kg
h₀          = 222.63 + 548.05 = 770.68 kJ/kg
            ->  T₀ = 752.8 K
```

Cross-check by interval-mean cp: 548.05/(752.8 − 222.65) = **1.0337 kJ·kg⁻¹·K⁻¹**,
between 1.003 at 222 K and about 1.10 at 750 K. Consistent.

| Model | cp [kJ·kg⁻¹·K⁻¹] | T₀ | T_aw (r = Pr^⅓ ≈ 0.89) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calorically perfect, γ = 1.4 | 1.0047 (const) | 768.1 K / 495.0 °C | 708.1 K / 435.0 °C |
| **Thermally perfect, h₀ = h + V²/2** | 1.0337 (mean) | **752.8 K / 479.7 °C** | **694.5 K / 421.4 °C** |

**Correction: −15.3 K on T₀ (−2.0 %).** At M 3.0 it was −5.7 K (−0.9 %). Still not
decisive, but now large enough that quoting the calorically perfect number without
the caveat would be sloppy rather than harmless.

Source for cp(T) and h(T): standard air ideal-gas property tables (Cengel A-17
lineage), **recited, [TO VERIFY]** against NIST-JANAF or NASA Glenn coefficients
before M2 closes. Two values recited from these tables already turned out wrong in
the M1 density cases, so this warning is not decorative.

### 3.3 Material survival — M5 becomes a decision, not a confirmation

| Material | Sustained limit | ρ [kg·m⁻³] | Verdict at T_aw = 421 °C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Al 2024-T3 / 7075 | ~120 °C | 2 780 | dead, by a factor of 3.5 |
| Al 2618 (RR58 — Concorde) | ~127 °C | 2 760 | dead |
| **Ti-6Al-4V** | ~350–400 °C (creep) | 4 430 | **MARGINAL — 20 to 70 K below T_aw** |
| Ti-6242S (high-temperature Ti) | ~540 °C | 4 540 | alive |
| Ti β (B-120VCA — SR-71) | ~500–550 °C | 4 850 | **alive — the answer the SR-71 arrived at** |
| 17-7PH stainless | ~430 °C | 7 800 | marginal, and +76 % density |
| Inconel 718 / René 41 | > 650 °C | 8 190 | leading edges, hot structure, engine |

> **This is what the re-baseline bought.** At M 3.0 the answer was robust:
> T_aw = 296 °C against a Ti-6Al-4V limit of 350–400 °C, a 55–105 K margin that the
> γ correction moved by only 4 K. M5 would have confirmed the obvious.
>
> At M 3.5, **T_aw sits 20–70 K ABOVE the Ti-6Al-4V sustained limit.** Whether the
> airframe can still be Ti-6Al-4V now depends entirely on where radiative
> equilibrium lands — which is precisely the calculation M5 performs. The module is
> promoted from confirming a conclusion to deciding one.
>
> **[TO PROVE, M5]** The expected answer is the one the SR-71 arrived at:
> β-titanium or Ti-6242S for hot structure, Ti-6Al-4V only where radiation keeps
> the skin cool, Inconel at leading edges. Not asserted until M5 computes the
> balance.

**Thermal growth (first order, redone in M5).** Ti alloys, α ≈ 8.6 × 10⁻⁶ K⁻¹.
Ground 288 K to cruise skin 620–695 K gives ΔT = 332–407 K:

```
ΔL/L = 2.9 to 3.5 × 10⁻³  =  2.9 to 3.5 mm per metre of airframe
```

Up from 1.9 mm/m at M 3.0. Per unit length because vehicle length is not yet
defined (§5.2).

## 5. Declared gaps

Deliberate deferrals. Each names the point at which it becomes blocking.

### 5.1 No acceleration corridor — blocking at M4 engine sizing

Everything above is a cruise snapshot. On a Mach 3.5 aircraft **the engine is not
sized by cruise, it is sized by the transonic pinch**: wave drag peaks around
M 1.0–1.2, where thrust margin is worst. A design point without the corridor
describes an aircraft that is already flying.

Representative schedule **[PLACEHOLDER — the schedule is an M6+M4 deliverable]**:

| M | h [m] | T∞ [K] | q [kPa] | T₀ [K] (γ = 1.4) | T₀ [K] (thermally perfect) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.90 | 11 000 | 216.65 | 12.83 | 251.7 | 251.7 (Δ < 0.1 K) |
| 1.20 | 12 000 | 216.65 | 19.49 | 279.0 | 279.0 (Δ < 0.1 K) |
| 2.00 | 18 000 | 216.65 | 21.01 | 390.0 | 389.9 (Δ = 0.1 K) |
| 3.50 | 26 000 | 222.65 | 18.46 | 768.1 | **752.8 (Δ = 15.3 K)** |

Two readings that survive the re-baseline:

- **q still peaks in the corridor, not at cruise** — 21.0 kPa at M 2.0 against
  18.5 at M 3.5. Structural loads are not set by the design point.
- **The calorically imperfect correction is negligible below M ≈ 2.5.** That bounds
  where ADR-000 D10 has to be taken seriously.

Location of min(T − D): **[TO DETERMINE, M6 + M4]**. Expected near M 1.1 ± 0.1.

### 5.2 No geometry — blocking at the start of M6

No length, no wing area, no mass class, no configuration. M6 (area rule,
Sears-Haack), M7 (mass fractions) and M8 (inertia tensor) are **unbuildable**
without them. **Intent: derive geometry from the physics, not assume it.**

What q gives, unchanged by the re-baseline because q itself is unchanged:

```
W/S = q · C_L
  C_L = 0.10  ->  W/S = 1.846 kPa =  188 kg/m²
  C_L = 0.15  ->  W/S = 2.769 kPa =  282 kg/m²
```

Back-calculating the SR-71 at its own cruise condition (M 3.2, 24 km → q = 21.0 kPa;
mid-cruise mass ≈ 55 t, S = 167.2 m² **[TO CITE]**) gives W/S = 3.23 kPa and
**C_L ≈ 0.154**. So C_L = 0.10 describes a lighter, larger-winged aircraft than the
SR-71; **282 kg/m² is the SR-71-like figure, not 188**. Carry both until M6 picks
one, and pick it for a stated reason.
